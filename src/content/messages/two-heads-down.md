A political network is built out of people who hold specific jobs. On August 18, Wyoming voters took two of the most important jobs in the Wyoming Freedom Caucus away in a single night.

John Bear chaired the caucus from 2022 to 2024. Chip Neiman served as Speaker of the Wyoming House in 2025 and 2026, the chamber's top leadership post. Both lost their primaries. Bear lost Campbell County's House District 31. Neiman lost the Senate District 1 race in Weston and Crook counties. [WyoFile reported both results, along with the roles each man held](https://wyofile.com/in-stunning-upset-wyoming-freedom-caucus-loses-most-of-its-house-seats-backslides-in-senate/).

They were not the only ones. WyoFile counted thirteen caucus-aligned House incumbents defeated statewide, six more caucus-aligned candidates lost Senate races, and the organization failed to pick up a single new House seat. Its expected strength fell from a working majority to roughly ten reliable votes, by WyoFile's count.

## What this doesn't mean

It doesn't mean the Wyoming Freedom Caucus is finished. Ten reliable votes is still a real bloc in a small Legislature, and the national network behind it isn't going anywhere on its own timeline. This isn't a victory lap.

## What it does mean

It means the caucus enters the next session without the two people who ran it. A chairman and a Speaker are not interchangeable seats. They're the people who set the agenda and count the votes. Wyoming voters removed both of them in the same primary, after looking at what two sessions under that leadership produced: a documented push for stricter voter-registration rules, a legal fight over the residency requirement it created, and dozens of priority bills carried through Freedom Caucus-aligned committees.

Power that isn't accountable to Wyoming doesn't stay in place because it's persistent. It stays in place because voters don't look closely enough, often enough. On August 18, Wyoming looked.
